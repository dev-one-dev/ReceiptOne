# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Freelancers and small business owners in Canada and the USA who need to track expenses and receipts for tax purposes. They're evaluating ReceiptOne on a landing page before installing the app — often stressed about tax season, skeptical of yet another finance tool, and deciding in minutes whether this looks credible enough to trust with their tax records. Two regional storefronts serve them: `/ca` (CRA context, beaver mascot, CAD pricing) and `/us` (IRS context, eagle mascot, USD pricing).

## Product Purpose

ReceiptOne is a tax-ready expense tracking and receipt management app. This repository is the public marketing site that converts visitors into app installs/signups: hero + video, feature highlights, pricing, FAQ, articles, and a community feature-voting widget. Success is a visitor trusting the product enough to download it and start scanning receipts.

## Brand Personality

**Trustworthy & precise.** Calm, exact, no-nonsense — this is a tool people trust with their tax records, so credibility outranks charm. The regional mascots (beaver/eagle) are a light, restrained territorial marker, not a cartoon lead character; they should never read as playful enough to undercut the seriousness of tax-record handling.

## Anti-references

- **Generic fintech/SaaS template**: cream background + navy text + gradient badges + stock hero illustration. ReceiptOne already has a defined system (see CLAUDE.md: `#f5f4f0` page bg, `#0d0d14` dark sections, orange `#f97316` accent) — don't drift toward the templated fintech look.
- **Cluttered, dated accounting software**: dense spreadsheet-like layouts, QuickBooks-era visual noise. Keep information hierarchy clean even where the content (pricing tables, FAQ) tempts density.
- **Overly playful/cartoonish**: especially the beaver/eagle mascots. They're a regional accent, not a mascot-led brand — treat them with restraint so the page still reads as credible for handling tax records.

## Design Principles

1. **Credibility before charm.** Every section should read as precise and organized before it reads as delightful — this is a tool for tax records, not a consumer lifestyle app.
2. **Mascots are a regional touch, not a headline.** Beaver/eagle art stays supporting, restrained, and secondary to product substance and copy.
3. **Practice what you preach.** A tool that promises tax-ready organization should itself look organized — clean hierarchy, no visual clutter, no unjustified decoration.
4. **Stay off the fintech template.** Lean on the existing accent-orange-on-warm-neutral system rather than defaulting to cream+navy+gradient SaaS conventions.
5. **Two regions, one bar.** `/ca` and `/us` must feel like the same trustworthy product with a swapped mascot/currency/tax-authority context, not two divergent designs.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA baseline: contrast ratios (body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1), full keyboard navigation, and `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for scroll-reveal and interactive motion (tilt, spotlight, aurora effects) already used across the site.
